Practicum Overview

This page should be used in conjunction with the Project Dashboard to quickly access the GitLab repo and general information. The MCM practicum consists of 5 essential stages:

1. Proposal
2. Approval
3. Lit Review
4. Final Paper
5. Viva

Stage 1: Form a teammate, Secure a supervisor, and Draft a proposal

It is imperative that you identify a project partner as soon as possible, as the MCM practicum is strictly structured as a group project with a maximum of two members. Your partner can be from any MCM stream, giving you the flexibility to build a team with complementary skills and background expertise. Once your partnership is formed and your project direction is agreed upon, your team must immediately fork the project repository on GitLab using the following instructions.

You must fork your project from the Master MCM template. You should name your repo as 2027-mcm-username.

Important: You must fork your project before contacting any prospective supervisors. If this step is skipped, your repository remains invisible to staff, making it impossible for them to review your work or approve your supervision.

 

GitLab records are used as evidence of sustained work. Regular commits are expected, reflecting professional industrial software development standards.

Securing a staff supervisor is a foundational step in your practicum journey, and it is entirely your responsibility to have a supervisor confirmed prior to the Approval Panel presentation. Finalising a project direction often requires multiple meetings, and you may need to consult with several faculty members before finding the right academic fit for your domain. We strongly recommend beginning this outreach immediately to ensure ample time for faculty discussion and to avoid critical delays in your project timeline.

Mandatory: You must meet in person with a staff member to discuss your proposal. Use the template provided in your GitLab repo to prepare your proposal. Do not reach out to faculty members unless you have drafted an initial project proposal.

Once your team is formed and a potential supervisor is engaged, you must focus on refining your project scope and drafting a formal proposal. You may propose your own idea or select one from staff suggestions found here. Working closely with your prospective supervisor during this phase will help ensure your proposal meets all academic expectations and is fully prepared for panel evaluation.

Note: Students are responsible for any special hardware/software requirements unless provided by the supervisor.

Stage 2: Project Proposal Approval Panel

A template for your project proposal document is available by default within your GitLab repository. Your proposal must clearly articulate the research gap, core objectives, and technical methodology, alongside your planned experiments and evaluation metrics.

Ensure your final document is saved in PDF format in the docs/proposal directory well before the submission deadline, as it will be automatically fetched from your repository at the time of examination.

You must present your proposal to the Approval Panel within a strict 5-minute limit. Presentations are strictly verbal. No PowerPoint slides or visual decks are permitted. You will explain your project proposal directly and answer questions from the panel.

The Approval Panel timetable will be communicated to you via email.

Important: Proposals lacking an assigned supervisor one week in advance will not be allowed to present, resulting in non-approval.

Stage 3: Literature Review

While a formal literature review is not graded as a separate standalone deliverable for the MCM practicum, every group is required to complete one.

Conducting a literature review is an essential part of the research journey, and it directly constitutes a major assessment element for both your final paper and your viva.

Submit your literature review to your GitLab repo in the documentation directory.

Important: Failure to complete and submit an acceptable literature review will result in your Engagement mark being capped at a maximum of 5 (out of 10).

Stages 4: Final Paper & Viva

Students must submit a final paper that comprehensively reflects the work executed throughout the practicum.

The paper must be structured logically, beginning with an Introduction that establishes the background context and defines the core problem being addressed. This is followed by a Literature Review that critically evaluates existing work to highlight the identified research gap. Next, the Methodology section must detail your proposed solution and explain how it differs from current approaches, followed by a description of the Experimental Setup along with the specific metrics used to evaluate performance. The paper concludes with a Conclusion summarising key findings, limitations, and potential future work.

Following paper submission, a Viva will be scheduled where students must present their work and defend their findings to two independent examiners.

Important: The final paper and the viva are fully intertwined; you cannot have one without the other. Both components must be completed to fulfill this assessment requirement.

Final Paper

  • Max 10 pages (Double Column), including references. Papers exceeding the page limit requirement won't be marked.
  • Submit as PDF format to /docs/documentation

Viva Exam

  • 15-minute presentation
  • 30-minute questioning session
  • Two independent academic examiners

Marking Process

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is my practicum worth?

30 credits (equivalent to 4 modules). It contributes 20% to your final degree mark.

How much time should I devote?

Approximately 750 hours (25 hours per credit).

Will you find me a supervisor for my practicum?

While relevant staff members will be suggested for you to contact, securing a supervisor remains the student's responsibility. If the currently proposed projects do not align with your interests, you are encouraged to pitch your own idea to a faculty member whose research expertise matches your topic.

Will you tell me if my idea is a good one?

Topic suitability is something you will work out directly with your supervisor. Since refining a research plan can be a complex process involving multiple consultations, we strongly suggest you start these conversations early to ensure your idea is fully developed before the deadline.

What information should the approval form contain?

The approval form should provide a brief overview of your project, specifically detailing the problem you seek to address and your intended methodology. It should also outline the specific metrics to assess the performance of your approaches as well as providing a plan for how the workload will be distributed.

What happens if my project wasn't approved by the approval panel?

Following the Approval Panel, examiners will provide specific feedback detailing any areas that require revision. It is your responsibility to review these comments with your supervisor and address each point thoroughly. Once your supervisor is satisfied with the updated proposal, they will formally grant approval via the dashboard.

Do I hand up the code with the paper?

The code should be uploaded to your GitLab repo for the examiners to look at it if they wish to.
